Why Ashes

Memento mori isn't a new idea. The delivery is what's different.

Keeping death in view to live more deliberately goes back to Stoic philosophy. A few modern takes have tried to bring it into daily life — each gets one part right and misses another.

Push-notification apps

Send a handful of random alerts a day reminding you that you'll die.

Easy to mute, easy to dismiss without reading — it's an interruption competing with every other notification on your phone.

Printable "weeks of your life" posters

A grid of dots, one per week, printed once and hung on a wall.

Static. It stops updating the moment it's printed, and a wall you walk past every day is easy to stop seeing.

One-off death-clock sites

You visit, answer a few questions, get a number, close the tab.

A curiosity, not a habit. Nothing brings you back once the novelty wears off.

Ashes

Lives on the new tab page — the screen you already open dozens of times a day.

No interruption to dismiss. No wall to stop noticing. It's live, personalized to 30+ real factors, and it's already where your attention already goes.

The short version

Other approaches ask you to add a new habit — check an app, glance at a wall. Ashes uses a moment you already have, dozens of times a day, and turns it into the reminder instead.
