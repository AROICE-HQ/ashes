Ashes

Why Ashes Exists

A short reminder, on every new tab.

Memento Mori

"Ashes" refers to the old phrase "ashes to ashes, dust to dust" — a reminder of mortality. Memento mori, "remember you must die," is a practice at least as old as Stoic philosophy: keeping death in view, not to dwell on it, but to see the present moment more clearly.

Most days it's easy to forget that time is finite — nothing in a browser ever tells you otherwise. Ashes puts one honest number where you'll actually see it, and lets that number do the reminding.

"Let us prepare our minds as if we'd come to the very end of life. Let us postpone nothing."

— Seneca

Why the New Tab

A new tab is the most-opened, least-noticed screen in a browser — dozens of times a day, gone in an instant, holding no information of its own. Ashes uses that exact moment instead of adding another app to open or another notification to dismiss.

The countdown isn't a gimmick or a guess. It's calculated from your birthdate and an estimated lifespan you can refine with 30+ real health and lifestyle factors, and it updates live. Confronting that number isn't meant to be heavy — most people describe it as clarifying: a nudge to prioritize what actually matters and stop postponing what doesn't need to wait.

The Person Behind It

Ashes was built by Aryan Techie, founder of AROICE — out of a personal need for something that would turn mindless browsing into moments of reflection, rather than another tool demanding more attention.
